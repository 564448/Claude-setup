# Phase 2: schematic style

> Adapted 2026-10-30 from [sequoia-hope/easy_pcb](https://github.com/sequoia-hope/easy_pcb) `.claude/skills/pcb-design/references/schematic-style.md` @ 95bfe5e (MIT, © 2026 Sequoia Alexander). Edit freely; this copy no longer tracks upstream.

A schematic is a document for humans. Someone opening it should be able to follow power and signals from the connectors to the loads without searching. If it looks like it was generated by a script, it fails, no matter how correct the netlist is.

## Sheet organization

- **Small boards (< ~40 parts)**: one sheet, divided into labeled regions (graphic boxes with a title like "3.3 V BUCK").
- **Larger boards**: hierarchical sheets, one per functional block from the block diagram. The top sheet shows the blocks and how they connect.
- Order regions/sheets in signal-flow order: inputs and power entry left/first, processing in the middle, outputs right/last.
- Title block filled in: board name, revision, date, author, sheet title.

## Within a block

- **Flow left to right.** Inputs enter from the left, outputs leave to the right.
- **Voltage top to bottom.** Higher voltages toward the top, ground at the bottom. Power symbols point up, ground symbols point down. Never a ground symbol pointing up.
- **Wires, not labels, inside a block.** If two pins are on the same sheet and near each other, connect them with a wire. Labels are for connections between blocks or sheets, or to avoid a wire crossing a whole page.
- **Net labels for cross-block nets on one sheet; hierarchical or global labels only between sheets.** A global label used where a wire would do is a style failure.
- **Supporting parts sit next to the pin they serve.** Decoupling caps at the IC's power pin, pull-ups at the pin they pull, feedback divider next to FB, crystal and load caps at the oscillator pins. A short column of caps beside the IC is fine; a separate "decoupling" section across the page is not.
- **Minimize crossings and bends.** No wires through symbols or text. No four-way junctions (use two offset T's).
- **Spacing**: room to breathe. Values and references must not overlap anything and must be readable without zoom.

## Symbols

- Pins arranged by function, not by package pin number: inputs and control left, outputs right, power top, ground bottom. If the library symbol is pin-number ordered (common for MCUs and generated connectors), make a project-local symbol. For big MCUs, split into units (power, GPIO bank A, peripherals).
- Connectors: pin order matches the physical connector. Label each pin with its function next to it.
- Every part has a meaningful value: "10k", "100n 50V X7R", "DRV8353RS". Not "R" or "C".

## Annotations

Add short text notes for non-obvious choices ("Rsense 2 mΩ: 30 A peak, 1.8 W, see parts/rsense.md"), layout-critical things ("Place C12 within 2 mm of U3 pin 7"), DNP options and jumpers, test points, and rail voltages and current at the source.

## Net naming

Name every net that leaves a block or matters for routing: `+12V`, `+3V3`, `VBUS`, `MOTOR_A`, `I2C_SDA`, `SW_BUCK`. Unnamed auto-nets are fine only for local connections inside a block. These names feed `nets.csv` in phase 3.

## Checks

No style-check script exists yet. Check these by hand, or with an ad hoc script over the `.kicad_sch` (read-only S-expression parse):
- global labels connecting two pins close together on one sheet (should be a wire)
- symbols on a regular grid (origins clustering on a few evenly spaced x/y values)
- decoupling caps far from the IC power pin on their net
- ground symbols not pointing down, power symbols not pointing up
- overlapping text, text over wires
- missing value, footprint or MPN/LCSC fields

Scripts catch the mechanical failures; the visual review catches the rest.

## Visual review

Export the schematic to PDF/SVG and render to PNG. The reviewer answers:
1. Can I follow the main power path from input connector to every load?
2. Can I follow each signal from source to destination?
3. Does any region look like a grid or a list rather than a circuit?
4. Is every passive next to what it serves?
5. Is anything cramped, overlapping or hard to read?
6. Would a senior engineer be comfortable presenting this in a design review?

## Gate checklist (phase 2)

- [ ] ERC: 0 errors; every warning explained
- [ ] Style checks done (output or notes pasted)
- [ ] Every part cross-checked against its `parts/*.md` audit: required external parts present, values match calculations
- [ ] Every symbol has value, footprint, MPN, supplier PN fields
- [ ] Visual review passed (findings pasted)
